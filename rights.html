Delay Is Not Inevitable. The Law Agrees.

Speedy justice isn't a favor the system does for you — parts of it are enforceable rights. Here's what exists and how to invoke it.

1. Speedy Trial is a Fundamental Right (Article 21)

The Supreme Court held in Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar (1980) that unreasonable delay in trial violates the right to life and liberty. If you're an undertrial held beyond a reasonable period, courts can grant bail on delay grounds alone — no other conditions needed. Cited in thousands of bail orders since.

2. Default Bail (Section 167 CrPC)

If police don't file a charge sheet within 60 days (90 for serious offenses), the accused has an absolute right to default bail. This is mechanical — the court has no discretion if the deadline passed. Many undertrials don't know to demand it.

3. MGNREGA-Style Delay Compensation Exists in Civil Justice Too

The Commercial Courts Act and various procedural rules allow costs for adjournments. Under Order XVII CPC, a party causing repeated adjournments can be ordered to pay costs to the other side. Courts have become stricter post-2015 — ask your advocate to seek adjournment costs against delaying parties.

4. Consumer Commissions Have Statutory Deadlines

Consumer Protection Act rules require complaints to be decided within 3–5 months of the opposite party's notice. While overruns are common, citing the statutory timeline in your pleadings strengthens requests for priority listing.

5. Lok Adalat Reference Is Your Right Too

Any case can be referred to Lok Adalat at any stage — and you can request it. Awards are decrees of civil court (binding, appeal-free) and court fees are refunded when settled. For settlement-amenable disputes, this is often the fastest exit.

6. RTI: Demand Your Case's Delay Data

You can file an RTI asking the court registry: "How many hearings were adjourned in Case No. X, and at whose instance?" This creates an official record of delay — powerful in applications for early hearing or costs.

Escalation Ladder When Your Case Stalls

No hearing listed for 6+ months?

File a "listing application" through your advocate — or yourself if unrepresented. Registries must list cases with pending applications.

Repeated adjournments by the other side?

Move an application under Order XVII Rule 1 CPC seeking costs and a final-hearing date. Courts increasingly grant both.

Judge vacancy or transfer stalled everything?

Write to the District Judge / Registrar General. Administrative letters from litigants do get counted in court-management statistics.

Systemic inaction affecting many people?

Public interest litigation or a letter-petition to the High Court's administrative side can move entire backlogs. Legal aid societies (NALSA) help group filers.

Free legal help: NALSA (nalsa.gov.in) and every District Legal Services Authority provide free lawyers to those below income thresholds — and run Lok Adalats quarterly. No sign-up needed on this site; these are official government services.
